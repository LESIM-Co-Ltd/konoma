konoma parses, lays out and draws **every** Mermaid diagram itself — no browser,
no Node, no external renderer, nothing to install. A ```` ```mermaid ```` fence
in a Markdown file becomes a real picture in the terminal, and a standalone
`.mmd` file opens full screen.

![konoma showing samples/mermaid.md, with a sequence diagram and a ZenUML diagram drawn inline as real images](../../../assets/mermaid-gallery.png)

Every picture below was produced by that same renderer, from the fences in
[`samples/mermaid.md`](https://github.com/LESIM-Co-Ltd/konoma/blob/main/samples/mermaid.md),
at the shipped defaults (`ui.mermaid_theme = "dark"`, `ui.mermaid_curve = "basis"`).
The backgrounds are transparent — in a terminal the diagram sits on your own
background; here it sits on a dark plate.

## Two looks, one config line

The default look is **mermaid's own**: nothing changes unless you ask for it.
One line switches flowcharts and state diagrams to konoma's own orthogonal
router and designed palette:

```toml
[ui]
mermaid_routing = "konoma-orthogonal"
```

In that mode every edge bends only at right angles with sharp corners, decision
diamonds become chamfered rectangles so an edge can land on a flat face, a
straight-through edge across ranks aligns into a single unbent lane, and the
diagram switches to konoma's own palette — dark node fills, dashed subgraph
frames, and `classDef`/`style` colors carried consistently onto a node's border,
its edges and its label (so `ui.mermaid_theme` has no effect there).

It applies to **`flowchart` and `stateDiagram-v2` only**. Those two are shown
twice below — konoma-orthogonal first, then the default `splines` — and the
other 21 kinds are shown once, because routing never reaches them.

See [Configuration](../../reference/configuration/) for every Mermaid option, and
[Previews in depth](../preview/) for the keys that zoom, pan and open a diagram
full screen.

## Graphs

### `flowchart` — boxes and arrows

The workhorse: three quarters of the mermaid in the wild is this one kind. Note
the direction (`LR`), the stadium and diamond shapes, and the labels riding on
the arrows.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[0]} />

### Colour — `classDef`, `:::`, `style`, `linkStyle`

Colour is how a flowchart says what belongs together. `classDef` names a set of
declarations, `class` (or the `:::` shorthand) attaches it to nodes, `style`
paints one node directly, and `linkStyle` paints edges by declaration index.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[1]} />

### Size — a diagram is drawn at the size it needs

konoma lays a diagram out at the size of the text around it and never blows it
up. A busy one is genuinely wide — this is konoma's own preview dispatch, every
format it knows and the three ways a picture can reach a terminal.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[2]} />

### `stateDiagram-v2` — modes and transitions

konoma's whole UI is two modes and the moves between them. `[*]` is the start
and end marker, and a state can contain a state machine of its own — the frame
around `Preview` is drawn from the nesting, not from a hint.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[3]} />

### `classDiagram` — types and what they know

A class box has compartments: the name, then fields, then methods, with `+`/`-`
for visibility. `<|--` is inheritance and `-->` a plain association.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[4]} />

### `erDiagram` — entities and how many of each

The crow's-foot marks are the point: `||` is exactly one, `o{` is zero or more.
An entity can carry an attribute block with types and `PK`/`FK` keys.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[5]} />

## Interactions

### `sequenceDiagram` — who says what, in order

Time runs down the page: an actor, an activation bar (`+` … `deactivate`) for
the worker thread being busy, and an `alt` block for the branch every terminal
takes differently.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[6]} />

### `zenuml` — the same story, written as code

ZenUML describes an interaction as nested calls rather than as arrows. A block
with `{ … }` is the callee doing work, and `if`/`else` reads as it would in a
program.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[7]} />

## Trees and boards

### `mindmap` — one idea, branching

Written as indentation alone. konoma lays a mindmap out left to right rather
than radially, because a radial layout puts half the labels on the wrong side of
the line in a terminal.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[8]} />

### `kanban` — columns of cards

Columns come from the first indent level, cards from the second. `@{ … }`
attaches metadata to a card — assignee, priority, ticket.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[9]} />

## Time

### `journey` — how a task actually feels

Each step gets a score from 1 to 5, drawn as a face, and the people involved
after the score. Sections band the day.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[10]} />

### `timeline` — what happened when

Periods on the left, events on the right; `section` bands them. A period can
carry several events separated by `:`.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[11]} />

### `gantt` — bars against a calendar

Note the `section` bands, the `after` dependency that chains one bar to the end
of another, the `crit` tag, and the `milestone` drawn as a diamond.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[12]} />

## Structure

### `requirementDiagram` — what must hold, and what proves it

A requirement carries an id, a text, a risk and a verification method; an
element is the thing that satisfies or verifies it.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[13]} />

### `gitGraph` — branches and merges

Commits run along a lane; `branch` opens a new one, `checkout` moves between
them, and `merge` brings one back. Tags sit above the commit they mark.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[14]} />

### `C4Context` — systems, people and boundaries

The C4 vocabulary: a `Person`, a `System` we own, a `System_Ext` we do not, and
a boundary drawn around what is ours. Relations can carry a technology as a
second label.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[15]} />

### `block-beta` — a grid you place things on

Unlike a flowchart, `columns` decides the shape: it says how many cells a row
holds, `:n` widens a block across several, and a `block:` frame nests a grid
inside a cell.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[16]} />

### `architecture-beta` — services, groups and which side a wire leaves from

Every edge names the side it leaves and the side it arrives on (`R` right, `L`
left, `T` top, `B` bottom), so the picture is stated rather than inferred.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[17]} />

## Data

### `pie` — parts of a whole

`showData` prints the raw number beside each label as well as the share.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[18]} />

### `xychart-beta` — bars and lines on the same axes

Two series over one x-axis: a bar for what was measured, a line for what was
budgeted.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[19]} />

### `quadrantChart` — two axes, four verdicts

Points are placed by `[x, y]` in the unit square, and each quadrant gets a name.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[20]} />

### `radar-beta` — several things scored on the same axes

One closed curve per subject over shared axes — the shape is the comparison.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[21]} />

### `treemap-beta` — nested boxes sized by value

Area is the number. Indentation nests, so a section's box is as big as its
leaves put together.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[22]} />

### `packet-beta` — bit fields laid out in rows

Ranges are bit offsets, so a wide field really is drawn wide.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[23]} />

### `sankey-beta` — flow that splits and keeps its width

Comma-separated `source,target,value`. The band widths stay proportional as the
flow divides.

<MermaidCatalogItem lang="en" item={manifest.items[24]} />

## If konoma cannot draw it

Nothing is invented. A kind konoma does not know, or a syntax error, falls back
to the source shown as text — and `ui.mermaid = "text"` asks for the legacy
Unicode rendering everywhere.
